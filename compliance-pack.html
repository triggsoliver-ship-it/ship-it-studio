What changed, and why it lands on the clerk

The Practitioners’ Guide 2025 added Assertion 10, Digital and data compliance, to the Annual Governance Statement. It first applies to the 2025-26 return.

To sign “Yes” the council needs

  • Council email on a domain the council owns
  • A website that meets the accessibility rules, with a current accessibility statement
  • The documents the law requires, published on time
  • Data protection in order
  • An adopted IT policy covering members, staff and personal devices

What most councils are missing

  • A written IT policy that has been minuted
  • An accessibility statement reviewed since it was first written
  • One page showing the evidence behind each answer
  • A report that explains it to members in plain English

Questions clerks ask

Is this legal advice?

No. These are templates to adapt. They follow the Practitioners’ Guide 2025 and the Transparency Code for Smaller Authorities, but your council must check them against the current guidance and adopt its own version. If unsure, ask your county association or internal auditor.

Can the council pay for it?

Yes. You pay by card and Stripe emails a receipt you can put through as a normal expense.

Does it suit a very small council?

Yes. The Transparency Code checklist is written for councils with a turnover of £25,000 or less, and Assertion 10 applies to every smaller authority except parish meetings.

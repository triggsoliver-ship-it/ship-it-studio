Why quotes go quiet

Nobody decides to lose the job. The customer meant to reply and got busy. You meant to chase and got busy too. Whoever follows up first usually gets the answer.

FOLLOW-UP 1

A friendly nudge

A few days after the quote: did it arrive, and are there any questions?

FOLLOW-UP 2

The usual worry, answered

About a week later: a short note that deals with the thing customers most often hesitate over.

FOLLOW-UP 3

An easy way to say no

Two weeks on: a last, polite note. A clear no is worth having too, because it clears your list.

Straight answers

Is this cold email?

No. Sales Chaser only follows up people who asked you for a quote and received one. It does not contact anyone else.

Will it pester my customers?

No. Three short, polite follow-ups at most, on working days, and it stops as soon as they reply.

Does it change my prices or offer discounts?

Never. It follows up the quote you sent. Any question about price comes to you.

What does it cost?

Desk is free. The version that sends for you is in pilot, so there is no public price yet. Tell us roughly how many quotes you send a month and we will give you a fixed figure before you commit to anything.

What do I need to start?

A list of your open quotes: customer, value and the date you sent it. A spreadsheet is fine.
